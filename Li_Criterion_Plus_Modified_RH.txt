# Li's Criterion for the Riemann Hypothesis

*A well-posed replacement for the "Narrow Steep Cone Condition."*

> **Note:** Li's criterion is an **equivalence** with the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), not a proof of it. Establishing the hypothesis side unconditionally is as hard as RH itself.

---

## 1. Setup

The completed zeta function is entire:

$$\xi(s)=\tfrac12\, s(s-1)\,\pi^{-s/2}\,\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)$$

It satisfies:

- **Functional equation:** $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$
- **Real symmetry:** $\xi(s)=\overline{\xi(\bar s)}$

The zeros of $\xi$ are exactly the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$, denoted $\mathcal{Z}(\xi)$. They are symmetric about both the real axis and the line $\operatorname{Re}(s)=\tfrac12$.

## 2. Li Coefficients

For $n\ge 1$, with the sum taken over nontrivial zeros $\rho$ in conjugate pairs:

$$\lambda_n=\sum_{\rho}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{\rho}\right)^{n}\right]=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,\frac{d^{n}}{ds^{n}}\Big[s^{\,n-1}\log\xi(s)\Big]_{s=1}$$

## 3. Formal Statement (Li 1997; Bombieri–Lagarias 1999)

$$\Big(\xi \text{ entire}\ \land\ \xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\ \land\ \forall n\ge 1:\ \lambda_n\ge 0\Big)\iff \forall\rho\in\mathcal{Z}(\xi):\ \operatorname{Re}(\rho)=\tfrac12$$

## 4. Component Definitions

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\xi$ entire | Completed zeta function has no poles |
| $\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ | Reflection symmetry about $\sigma=\tfrac12$ (replaces the earlier "endomorphism" and nonstandard reflection condition) |
| $\lambda_n \ge 0$ | Positivity of all Li coefficients (replaces the "cone" condition) |
| $\mathcal{Z}(\xi)$ | Nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ |
| Conclusion | Every nontrivial zero lies on the critical line |

## 5. Why It Works

Define the map $\rho \mapsto w = 1-\dfrac1\rho$. Then

$$|w|=\frac{|\rho-1|}{|\rho|}=1 \iff \operatorname{Re}(\rho)=\tfrac12$$

so the critical line maps onto the unit circle.

- **If RH holds:** every $|w|=1$, so each term $1-w^n$ has nonnegative real part contribution and $\lambda_n>0$.
- **If RH fails:** a zero off the line has a partner $1-\rho$ with $|w|>1$. Its contribution grows exponentially in $n$ and rotates in phase, forcing $\lambda_n<0$ for some $n$.

## 6. Known Results

- $\lambda_1 = 1+\dfrac{\gamma}{2}-\dfrac12\log 4\pi \approx 0.0231 > 0$
- $\lambda_n>0$ has been verified numerically for a large range of $n$.
- Under RH: $\lambda_n \sim \dfrac{n}{2}\left(\log n - 1 + \gamma - \log 2\pi\right)$
- Unconditionally proving $\lambda_n\ge 0$ for all $n$ is equivalent to RH.

## 7. Equivalent Forms

**Zero-location form.** Under the map $w=1-1/\rho$:

$$\forall\rho\in\mathcal{Z}(\xi):\ \left|1-\frac1\rho\right|=1\iff \forall\rho\in\mathcal{Z}(\xi):\ \operatorname{Re}(\rho)=\tfrac12$$

**Related criteria.**

- Robin's inequality (for the divisor function)
- de Bruijn–Newman constant: $\Lambda\ge 0$ is proven (Rodgers–Tao, 2018); $\Lambda\le 0$ is equivalent to RH

## 8. Relation to the Original "Cone Condition"

The original condition $E(\sigma)=\alpha\left|\sigma-\tfrac12\right|$ with $\alpha\gg1$ was never defined in terms of $\zeta$, so it effectively assumed the conclusion. Li's criterion fixes this by replacing it with a precise, checkable hypothesis, $\lambda_n \ge 0$ for all $n$. The difficulty is not removed, only moved to proving that positivity.
